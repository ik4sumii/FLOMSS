FLOMSS: Generative Music Source Separation by Flow Matching

Anonymous Authors

📄 arXiv 💻 Code
Abstract: Music source separation (MSS) has long been dominated by discriminative models that struggle to balance distortion and perceptual quality under the wide bandwidth, greater timbral diversity, and heavier spectral overlap inherent to music. Recently, flow matching has emerged as a strong generative paradigm that theoretically better models complex data distribution. We present a systematic study of flow matching for MSS, establishing an open-source generative framework that operates in full-band stereo format. Specifically, we investigate prediction targets, flow paths, source distribution factorization, and mixture consistency constraints, introducing mixture-consistent flow to MSS for the first time. Beyond standard Signal-to-Distortion Ratio (SDR) evaluation, we further benchmark MSS models with spectral distances and perceptual distances using multiple embeddings, providing empirical evidence of the perception–distortion tradeoff in MSS. On the MUSDB18-HQ dataset, our best model, based on a 16M-parameter NCSN++ backbone, surpasses both prior generative approaches and a predictive baseline of identical architecture on spectral and perceptual metrics, while remaining competitive with state-of-the-art methods. Our results demonstrate that flow matching is an effective paradigm for generative MSS that yields perceptually competitive separations with minimal architectural and training complexity.
